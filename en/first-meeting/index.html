New to A.A.?

If you have a problem with drinking, there are people in A.A. who have been where you are.

What is A.A.?

Alcoholics Anonymous is a fellowship of people who share their experience, strength and hope with each other that they may solve their common problem and help others to recover from alcoholism.

The only requirement for membership is a desire to stop drinking. There are no dues or fees for A.A. membership; we are self-supporting through our own contributions. A.A. is not allied with any sect, denomination, politics, organization or institution; does not wish to engage in any controversy, neither endorses nor opposes any causes. Our primary purpose is to stay sober and help other alcoholics to achieve sobriety.

Am I an alcoholic?

Only you can answer that. A.A. has twelve questions that may help. A.A. does not make diagnoses.

What happens at a meeting?

The Preamble is read. Members share their experience: what it was like, what happened, and what it is like now. No one interrupts. The meeting closes with the Serenity Prayer. It lasts about an hour.

You do not have to speak. If asked, give your first name. You do not have to say you are an alcoholic.

What does it cost?

Nothing. At some meetings a basket is passed for the group's expenses. Contributions are voluntary.

Open or closed meeting?

Closed meetings are for alcoholics or anyone who may have a drinking problem. Open meetings are for anyone.

Who will know?

No one. First names only. There are no attendance records. What is said at the meeting stays at the meeting.

Is A.A. religious?

No. A.A. is not allied with any religion. Members hold every belief and none.

Where are the meetings?

There are 17 English-speaking meetings a week in El Poblado, Laureles, Belén and Sabaneta, and on Zoom. There are also 138 Spanish-speaking groups in the metropolitan area; most meet every evening at 7:00 p.m.
